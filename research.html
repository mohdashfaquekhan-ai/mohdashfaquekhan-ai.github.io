Our Science

Research

We investigate the sustainable conversion of lignocellulosic biomass into fuels, enzymes, chemicals and functional materials through integrated biochemical, microbial and process-engineering approaches.

Research Philosophy

From Biomass Recalcitrance to Integrated Biorefinery Solutions

Lignocellulosic biomass represents one of the world's most abundant renewable carbon resources, yet its complex and recalcitrant architecture limits efficient conversion into useful products.

LignoBio Lab develops integrated strategies to overcome this recalcitrance through biomass pretreatment, enzymatic deconstruction, fermentation, lignin valorisation and sustainable process design.

Our long-term objective is to move beyond single-product biomass processing toward complete utilisation of cellulose, hemicellulose and lignin within circular biorefinery systems.

01
Biomass Deconstruction

Biomass Pretreatment & Fractionation

Effective pretreatment is a critical step in lignocellulosic biorefineries because the cellulose–hemicellulose–lignin matrix restricts enzyme accessibility and downstream conversion.

Our research evaluates physicochemical and integrated pretreatment strategies capable of disrupting biomass architecture while maximising carbohydrate recovery and reducing undesirable degradation products.

Alkaline pretreatment Microwave-assisted pretreatment Surfactant-assisted fractionation Deep eutectic solvents Delignification Biomass structural analysis
02
Biological Catalysis

Enzyme Technology & Biocatalysis

Enzymes provide highly selective and environmentally compatible routes for biomass deconstruction and pollutant transformation.

We study the production, characterisation, optimisation and application of microbial enzymes involved in lignocellulose degradation and environmental biotechnology.

Cellulases Xylanases Pectinases Laccases Enzyme immobilisation Biocatalyst optimisation
03
Renewable Energy

Second-Generation Biofuels

Agricultural residues can provide renewable alternatives to fossil-derived fuels without directly competing with conventional food resources.

Our work focuses particularly on the development and optimisation of lignocellulosic bioethanol processes integrating pretreatment, enzymatic saccharification and microbial fermentation.

Lignocellulosic bioethanol Enzymatic saccharification Fermentation optimisation Simultaneous saccharification & fermentation Fermentable sugar recovery Process intensification
04
Biomass Valorisation

Lignin & Cellulose Valorisation

Complete biorefinery development requires utilisation of lignin and cellulose as valuable feedstocks rather than treating residual biomass fractions as low-value waste.

We are interested in developing routes for transforming these renewable biopolymers into nanomaterials, adsorbents, functional materials and other high-value products.

Lignin valorisation Nanocellulose Nanolignin Biomass-derived adsorbents Functional biomaterials Value-added bioproducts
05
Environmental Solutions

Environmental Biotechnology

Biological systems and enzyme-based technologies offer sustainable alternatives for treatment of industrial pollutants and contaminated water.

Our research investigates microbial, enzymatic and nano-biocatalytic strategies for pollutant degradation, wastewater treatment and environmental remediation.

Enzymatic dye degradation Laccase-based remediation Wastewater treatment Nano-biocatalysis Pollutant removal Sustainable remediation
06
Systems Integration

Sustainable Biorefineries

Laboratory-scale process performance alone does not determine whether a biomass technology can become commercially and environmentally viable.

We therefore seek to integrate experimental bioprocess development with process modelling, resource utilisation, techno-economic assessment and life-cycle thinking.

Integrated biorefineries Process optimisation Mass & energy balance Techno-economic assessment Life-cycle assessment Circular bioeconomy
