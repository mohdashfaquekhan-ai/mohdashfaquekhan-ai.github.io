Our Science

Research

LignoBio Lab investigates the sustainable deconstruction and valorisation of lignocellulosic biomass through integrated chemical, biochemical, microbial and process-engineering approaches. Our research aims to transform renewable biomass into fuels, enzymes, chemicals and functional materials within sustainable biorefinery systems.

Research Philosophy

From Biomass Recalcitrance to Complete Resource Valorisation

Lignocellulosic biomass is an abundant renewable carbon resource, but its complex association of cellulose, hemicellulose and lignin limits efficient biological conversion. Overcoming this structural recalcitrance remains central to the development of economically viable lignocellulosic biorefineries.

Our research integrates biomass fractionation, physicochemical pretreatment, microbial enzyme production, enzymatic hydrolysis, fermentation and downstream valorisation to improve recovery and utilisation of individual biomass fractions.

Rather than focusing exclusively on a single product such as ethanol, our long-term objective is to establish integrated strategies in which cellulose, hemicellulose and lignin are directed toward complementary fuels, chemicals and functional materials.

Research Portfolio

Major Research Themes

Our research portfolio spans the complete lignocellulosic biomass conversion chain, from feedstock deconstruction to biological conversion, product development and sustainability assessment.

01 Biomass Deconstruction

Biomass Pretreatment & Fractionation

We develop and evaluate pretreatment strategies capable of disrupting the recalcitrant lignocellulosic matrix while improving cellulose accessibility, removing or redistributing lignin and preserving fermentable carbohydrate fractions. Particular emphasis is placed on integrating physicochemical treatments with subsequent enzymatic conversion.

Alkali Pretreatment Microwave Processing Surfactant-Assisted Pretreatment Deep Eutectic Solvents Delignification Biomass Characterisation
02 Biological Catalysis

Enzyme Technology & Biocatalysis

Our enzymology programme investigates microbial enzymes involved in lignocellulose deconstruction and environmental biotechnology. Research includes enzyme production, optimisation, purification, characterisation, immobilisation and application of biocatalysts under process-relevant conditions.

Cellulases Xylanases Pectinases Laccases Enzyme Immobilisation Nanobiocatalysis
03 Renewable Fuels

Second-Generation Biofuels

We investigate conversion of agricultural and lignocellulosic residues into second-generation bioethanol through integrated pretreatment, enzymatic hydrolysis and fermentation. The work addresses both carbohydrate accessibility and efficient microbial conversion of biomass-derived sugars.

2G Bioethanol Saccharification Fermentation SSF Pentose Utilisation Process Optimisation
04 Biomass Valorisation

Lignin & Cellulose Valorisation

A central objective of our emerging biorefinery research is to move beyond carbohydrate-only utilisation. We investigate strategies for recovering and transforming lignin and cellulose fractions into nanomaterials, adsorbents, functional biomaterials and other high-value renewable products.

Lignin Valorisation Nanocellulose Nanolignin Functional Materials Biomass-Derived Adsorbents Advanced Bioproducts
05 Environmental Solutions

Environmental Biotechnology

We apply microbial systems, oxidative enzymes, immobilised biocatalysts and nano-enabled biological approaches to environmental remediation. Current interests include degradation of synthetic dyes, pollutant removal and development of biomass-derived materials for wastewater treatment.

Dye Degradation Laccase Remediation Wastewater Treatment Nano-Biocatalysts Pollutant Removal Environmental Remediation
06 Systems Integration

Sustainable Biorefineries

We aim to connect experimental biomass conversion with whole-process evaluation. This includes integration of multiple biomass fractions, optimisation of material flows and assessment of technological feasibility through techno-economic and life-cycle perspectives.

Integrated Biorefineries Mass Balance Process Modelling Techno-Economic Assessment Life-Cycle Assessment Circular Bioeconomy

Emerging Directions

Where Our Research Is Moving

Our research programme continues to evolve toward higher-value utilisation of all lignocellulosic biomass fractions.

Direction 01

Integrated Feedstock Biorefineries

Development of integrated processes in which carbohydrate conversion to biofuels is combined with recovery and valorisation of lignin and residual biomass fractions.

Direction 02

Advanced Biomass-Derived Materials

Exploration of lignin, cellulose and other biomass-derived fractions as precursors for functional materials, adsorbents and environmental remediation systems.

Direction 03

Sustainable Process Evaluation

Integration of experimental process development with mass balance, techno-economic assessment and life-cycle evaluation to identify practically scalable biorefinery routes.
